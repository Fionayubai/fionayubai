Hi, my name is Fiona Wu! I’m from Beijing, China, and currently study Radio/Television/Film and Economics at Northwestern University. I work across mediums, from shooting on film cameras in heritage towns to experimenting with AI-generated voices and dialogue. For me, the form and process of making media can be just as impactful as the story itself.

01 / Moving image

Filmmaking.

Projects made through editing, directing, production design, and documentary.

Boxes Editing

Boxes follows a young girl experiencing grief for the first time, using boxes as a symbol for how young people process and contain difficult emotions. The film reflects on how contemporary society often encourages people to suppress or obscure grief amid the fast pace of everyday life.

Production Design

A story about a girl navigating a gap year, searching for work, and reconnecting with her cousin in a small Shanghai apartment. For a 48 Hour Film Competition in Shanghai, I designed the bedroom, bathroom, and kitchen to reflect their quirky, feminine, and messy lives.

System Error Directing

System Error is a romantic comedy that uses AI dubbing to explore love, technology, and human connection.

Choices Documentary

Choices explores a young woman’s life choice to move from a city in China to a rural area. Through intimate documentary storytelling, the film reflects on identity, place, and belonging.

City Soul Documentary

City Soul explores the everyday life and character of Beijing’s hutongs through the people, spaces, and memories that shape them. The documentary looks at how a changing city preserves its sense of community and identity.

02 / Experiments in form

Multimedia
projects.

Work that unfolds across image, sound, space, and interaction.

In Transit Time-based media

In Transit transforms an airplane cabin and drifting sky into a suspended, in-between world, reflecting my sense of dislocation between places and histories. Nonlinear fragments from Manchu films, images of my own face, and the sound of my breathing explore the uncertainty of belonging, before moving toward a sense of grounding and finding connection across places.

Research / Visual Essay

Whispers before
Vanishing

Whispers before Vanishing explores how Manchu cultural identity has transformed through history, film, visual culture, and personal memory. Through historical research, interviews, and visual analysis, the project asks how cultural identity can persist even when its most visible forms begin to disappear.

Research Question

How has Manchu cultural identity transformed over time, and how can its less visible traces still be found in contemporary Chinese life?
